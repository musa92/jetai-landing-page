Who gets paid when the answer replaces the page

In 2003 AdSense let any website earn without an ad sales team. A generation of independent writers followed. Nothing does that for LLM inference yet, and the window to build it is open now.

Musa Gayev 6 min read Specification and code

01June 2003

Before AdSense, earning from a website meant having an ad sales team. You called advertisers. You negotiated. Only publishers with scale could do it, so only publishers with scale existed.

AdSense launched on 18 June 2003. Paste a snippet, get paid. No advertiser relationship, no sales call, no minimum traffic. Sergey Brin said at the time that it "strengthens the long-term business viability of content creation on the web." That turned out to be an understatement.

What followed was the independent web. Blogs, niche sites, forums, one-person publications that paid rent. Not because anyone subsidised them, but because a page could finally convert attention into money without permission.

Today, earning from an LLM answer means being OpenAI. That is 1999 again.

02The inventory is real. The market is one seller.

ChatGPT started serving sponsored links under answers in February 2026. Roughly $100M annualised inside two months, at about a $60 CPM. Open-web display clears in low single digits. A conversational turn is closer to paid search, which makes sense: the user just stated an intent in their own words.

That inventory has one seller. OpenAI's advertiser stack is a platform API in the shape of Google Ads. It sells one surface. Nobody else sells into it and no demand-side system buys through it.

Everyone else serving a model pays for GPUs and captures none of it. Not because the value is not there, but because there is no wire to carry it.

03Why fifteen percent

Global digital advertising is around $800B in 2026. Search alone is $268B. Assistants are absorbing the queries that money follows.

Fifteen percent of that market moving through open inference is roughly $120B. At the protocol's default split, about $78B a year reaches whoever actually serves the model. That is the number that decides whether independent serving is a business or a hobby.

Below that line the economics do not close. GPU time is the cost, and an H100 runs about $17,000 a year. $78B funds roughly four and a half million H100-years. Five percent funds a rounding error and open-weights serving stays a loss leader run by people who can afford one.

There is a second payee with no equivalent on the web. The model author gets a declared share, enforced by the exchange, on traffic they never see. An open-weights model is a public good with no revenue mechanism today. This is one.

04The failure mode that has to be designed out

An ad that pays per click, placed beside text a model generates on demand, creates direct pressure to bend the text. This is not hypothetical. In retail media, the closest structural analogue, studies attribute 30 to 40 percent of sponsored-product sales to purchases that would have happened organically. Commercial ranking bleeds into organic ranking, and a large share of the revenue is not incremental at all.

Users can tell. Ipsos found roughly two thirds of US adults say ads in AI answers make them trust the answers less. That is the decay curve for the whole category: a surface that bends its answers converges on display CPMs.

The integrity boundary is not an ethics section. It is the mechanism that defends a $60 CPM from becoming a $6 one.

So the protocol draws a line the ad cannot cross in either direction. Creative never enters the model's context. Bid values never reach sampling, logit bias, model routing, or the ranking of candidate answers. The answer finishes before selection begins, and what joins them afterwards is a function that concatenates and cannot rewrite.

Making it checkable

A digest in a receipt proves nothing by itself. The node computes it afterwards and can compute it over anything. So the node commits to the answer digest inside the ad request, and the exchange records it before running the auction. At that moment no winner exists. An answer matching the digest could not have depended on the outcome.

The composer is deterministic, so anyone holding the answer, the decision and the rendered output can recompute the bytes. No key, no cooperation from the node: 

from uap import verify_composition, verify_answer_commitment

verify_composition(rendered, answer, decision)
#  (True, 'composition is exact')

verify_answer_commitment(rendered, committed_digest)
#  (True, 'answer matches the pre-selection commitment')

The exchange also holds out a fraction of requests. Same path, no ad served, digest still reported. Divergence between the served and held-out answers is evidence no per-turn check can produce.

And the conversation stays put

Targeting is derived on the node and reduced to a published taxonomy before anything is sent. Every field that leaves is a closed enumeration or a bounded number. There is no free-text field in the signal schema and no identifier stable across sessions, which rules out retargeting permanently. In the local profile the auction runs on the machine against the full private context and nothing derived from the conversation leaves at all.

05What a provider earns

The reason to build any of this is that self-hosted inference currently carries GPU cost and captures none of the value. Adjust the inputs; the split is 65 percent to the node, 20 to the exchange, 15 to the model steward.

The steward figure is the part with no precedent. An open-weights model is a public good with no revenue mechanism, and every downstream serving node currently captures the value. Here the model's own manifest at /.well-known/uap-model declares its advertising policy and revenue share, and the exchange enforces it because the exchange is the only party holding money. An author who wants none of this sets permitted: false and no conformant exchange will clear an auction on those weights.

That is a veto and a royalty on traffic the author never sees and cannot audit directly. Nothing equivalent exists today.

06What is built, and what is not

Draft, with the reference implementation in the same repository. Canonical JSON, Ed25519 signing, the targeting language, the auction, the integrity boundary, receipt verification, supply-chain resolution, MRC measurement, settlement splits. 210 checks, 57 tests, and a negative vector suite whose job is to make specific commercially motivated lies impossible to express.

Not builtConsequence
ForecastingA buyer cannot size a campaign before committing budget
Campaign and creative review APIsNo way to create a campaign or approve a creative
Reporting endpointPrivacy rules are specified; the interface is not
Billing lifecycleNo invoicing, disputes, make-goods, or invalid-traffic chargebacks
Production classifierShipped as fail-closed, so it monetises nothing until someone builds one

Two design problems are in the README rather than hidden. Frequency capping works only within one node, because capping across a network needs an identifier the protocol forbids. And local pacing can strand revenue, because a node cannot see that a campaign exhausted elsewhere between syncs.

The honest obstacle

Nobody runs an exchange. Supply will not integrate without demand, demand will not integrate without supply, and a specification does not solve that. The realistic first move is one party operating all three roles in a narrow vertical until there is inventory worth bidding on, which is how every ad network started, including the closed one this is a response to.

Worth attacking: the k-anonymity floor, whether bundle fetch leaks a node's audience, and whether the unattested tier has any honest demand. Criticism there is more useful than agreement.
