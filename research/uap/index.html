Turbnetic Research  ·  Note 01

Who gets paid when the answer replaces the page

Abstract In 2003 AdSense let any website earn without an ad sales team, and the independent web followed. Nothing does that for LLM inference. Assistants now carry advertising, the system selling it has one seller, and everyone else serving a model pays for compute and captures none of the value. This note specifies the missing layer: how a sponsored unit is selected and paid for on a turn, on a node nobody trusts, without the conversation leaving the machine and without the ad being able to move the answer.
Musa Aghayev  ·  September 2026  ·  github.com/musa92/uap
01Precedent

June 2003

Before AdSense, earning from a website meant having an ad sales team. Only publishers with scale could do it, so only publishers with scale existed.

AdSense launched on 18 June 2003.1 Paste a snippet, get paid. No advertiser relationship, no traffic minimum. It did not make writers rich. It made publishing cheap enough to keep doing, and the independent web followed.

The same trade is available now, and it is not that model providers get paid. It is that serving cost falls toward zero.

ChatGPT began serving sponsored links under answers in February 2026: roughly $100M annualised inside two months, at about a $60 CPM.2 A conversational turn prices like paid search, because the user has just stated an intent in their own words. That inventory has one seller. Everyone else pays for the compute out of pocket.

02The unit

What a sponsored turn looks like

The unit is deliberately unremarkable: the answer, a hard separator, then a labelled block the model never saw. Everything interesting is in what cannot happen, not in what is rendered.

03Unit economics

What a turn costs, and what it earns

Both sides of this are calculable from the hardware up, so there is no reason to argue about it.

Cost. A 70B-class open model, fp8, batched, on an H100 at about $2 an hour, sustains roughly 3,000 output tokens per second. A turn of ~400 output tokens costs $0.000074 to decode; ~1,200 input tokens add about a quarter of that in prefill. Call it $0.000093 a turn, or nine cents per thousand turns.

Revenue. At a CPM of p, one ad every L turns, and the node keeping 65 percent, revenue is 0.65p / 1000L per turn. Setting that equal to cost gives a break-even CPM of 0.143 × L: $0.43 at one ad every three turns, $1.42 at one in ten, $2.85 at one in twenty.

The market pays about $60.

It was never whether advertising can pay for inference. It is whether the revenue can reach the person running the hardware.

Which is worth stating plainly, because it changes what the problem is. A serving operator does not need a large share of the ad market. They need roughly two orders of magnitude less than the unit economics already support, and they need it without building an ad sales business, a billing system, a fraud team, and a demand relationship. That is a plumbing problem, and plumbing is what a protocol is for.

The second payee

An open-weights model is a public good with no revenue mechanism, and every downstream node captures the value today. Here the model's own manifest at /.well-known/uap-model declares its advertising policy and revenue share, and the exchange enforces it because the exchange holds the money. An author who wants none of it sets permitted: false and no conformant exchange will clear an auction on those weights.

04Integrity

The failure mode that has to be designed out

An ad that pays per click, beside text a model generates on demand, creates direct pressure to bend the text. In retail media, the closest structural analogue, studies attribute 30 to 40 percent of sponsored-product sales to purchases that would have happened anyway.4 Commercial ranking bleeds into organic ranking and much of the revenue is not incremental.

Users notice. Ipsos found roughly two thirds of US adults say ads in AI answers make them trust the answers less. That is the decay curve for the category: a surface that bends its answers converges on display CPMs.

The integrity boundary is not an ethics section. It is what defends a $60 CPM from becoming a $6 one.

Making it checkable

A digest in a receipt proves nothing alone, because the node computes it afterwards. So the node commits to the digest inside the ad request and the exchange records it before the auction. The composer being deterministic means anyone can recompute the bytes, with no key and no cooperation from the node: 

from uap import verify_composition, verify_answer_commitment

verify_composition(rendered, answer, decision)
#  (True, 'composition is exact')

The exchange also holds out a deterministic fraction of requests: same path, no ad served, digest still reported. Divergence between the served and held-out arms is evidence no per-turn check can produce.

05Roadmap

Where it goes next

Draft-01 covers the serve-time path end to end: canonical JSON, Ed25519 signing, the targeting language, the auction, the integrity boundary, receipt verification, supply-chain resolution, MRC measurement, and settlement splits. The conformance suite is mostly negative vectors. Its job is to make specific commercially motivated lies impossible to express.

What comes next is the buy side, in roughly this order. Each is a tracked milestone rather than a gap discovered later.

Two design problems are tracked openly rather than hidden. Frequency capping works only within one node, because capping across a network needs an identifier the protocol forbids. And local pacing can strand revenue, because a node cannot see that a campaign exhausted elsewhere between syncs. Both need a design, not a patch.

The honest obstacle

Nobody runs an exchange. Supply will not integrate without demand, demand will not integrate without supply, and a specification does not solve that. The realistic first move is one party operating all three roles in a narrow vertical until there is inventory worth bidding on, which is how every ad network started, including the closed one this responds to.

Worth attacking: the k-anonymity floor, whether bundle fetch leaks a node's audience, and whether the unattested tier has any honest demand. Criticism there is more useful than agreement.
