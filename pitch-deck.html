Two Compounding Problems

Turbines Fail Predictably.
Mechanics Don't Know What to Do.

Problem 1

Failures Are Predicted Too Late

  • 50,000+ utility-scale gas turbines generate 23% of global electricity
  • 68% of critical failures show sensor warning signatures 2+ weeks before breakdown — yet nobody catches them
  • Average post-failure diagnosis time: 72 hours of downtime, $50K–$2.4M per incident in parts, labor, and lost generation
  • 3–5% heat rate degradation per year from undetected compressor fouling — $1–2M lost per turbine annually
  • 75% of the global installed base predates 2005 — running on aging controls with zero AI integration
Problem 2

The Knowledge Is Locked Away

  • GE Frame 7 maintenance manual: 2,000+ pages. A new mechanic cannot be expected to know it
  • Experienced turbine engineers are retiring faster than they can be replaced — the knowledge walks out the door
  • When an alert fires today, the mechanic gets a code. Not a cause. Not a next step. Not a procedure reference
  • Result: wrong diagnoses, delayed repairs, escalated failures, and facilities entirely dependent on a shrinking pool of senior staff
  • New hires take 2–4 years to develop independent judgment on turbine anomalies

The core insight: The sensor data to predict every failure already exists. The procedure to respond correctly already exists in the manuals. Nobody has built the intelligence layer that connects both — in real time, in plain language, to the person holding the wrench.

The Solution

One Platform.
Three Layers of Intelligence.

Turbnetic.ai connects to existing sensors, learns continuously from fleet data, and closes both gaps simultaneously — early anomaly detection and precise maintenance guidance — in a single platform any operator can use from day one.

Layer 01

Predictive Anomaly Detection

Continuously monitors 200–800 sensor channels per turbine. AI model trained on millions of fleet-hours surfaces failure precursors 2–5 weeks before breakdown — not after it.

Industrial IoT Is Finally Mainstream

OPC-UA adoption has crossed 70% of new industrial control system deployments as of 2025. Historian platforms like OSIsoft PI now have cloud-native APIs. The connectivity layer that was the primary barrier to industrial AI in 2015 is now the default, not the exception.
